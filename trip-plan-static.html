Route & Distance Ledger

The shape of the trip

A closed loop from Delhi, anticlockwise through eastern, southern and western Rajasthan, closing back through the north. Seven overnight bases, no road driven twice except the 27 km Mount Abu ghat.

The rhythm alternates deliberately: two heavy days, a light day, two moderate days, the heaviest day, a light day, two heavy days home. Days 3 and 7 — Chittorgarh and Jaisalmer — are your rest days and also the two best days of the trip.

Timed for a Saturday start and a Sunday-afternoon finish. Day 1 leaves Delhi 05:30 Saturday; the return leg reaches Delhi ~14:30 the following Sunday, in daylight. Three things make that work: Jaipur is cut to 70 minutes, Junagarh Fort moves from Sunday morning to Saturday evening, and Saturday's Jaisalmer departure is pulled forward to 08:15. Nothing is lost — Junagarh is still seen.
Jaipur and Udaipur are deliberately minimised. Jaipur is a two-hour lunch stop. Udaipur is a single night and droppable. The days they freed went to Chittorgarh and Jaisalmer.

Three is the efficient number for this trip. Fuel and tolls divide by three instead of two, and most Indian hotels price a third person as an extra bed at ₹800–1,200 rather than a second room. Per-head cost drops roughly 22% against the same trip for two.

Day by Day

The Non-Vegetarian Map

What to order, where, and where you simply cannot. Rajasthani meat cooking is mutton-led; chicken is common but the state dishes are goat.

A note on heat. Authentic laal maas is built on Mathania red chillies and no turmeric, and it is properly hot — not restaurant-hot, actually hot. If one of the three of you is heat-sensitive, order safed maas (white, cashew-and-yoghurt, mild) alongside it rather than asking them to tone the laal maas down, which ruins it.
